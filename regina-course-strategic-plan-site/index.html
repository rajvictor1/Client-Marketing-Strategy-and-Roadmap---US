Pricing insight

Ruzuku reports that cohort-based creative arts courses with live critique sit in the upper premium range because personalized editorial attention is irreplaceable. Regina's live teaching, agent-level feedback, and publishing industry access justify a premium price.

Recommended pricing

  • Founding cohort: $1,295 (not $795 — too low for her authority)
  • Standard cohort after validation: $1,695 to $1,995
  • Premium tier with 1-on-1 manuscript review: $2,995+

Offer architecture

The Complete Revenue System

The flagship cohort is the starting point, not the entire business. Multiple revenue layers protect Regina's time and increase lifetime value.

Business Model

Revenue streams

1. Live cohorts — primary revenue 2. Self-paced recorded course — launched after 2–3 successful cohorts 3. Premium manuscript review / query package — upsell for high-intent students 4. Group coaching continuity — monthly membership for alumni 5. Corporate / institutional licensing — organizations that want memoir training

Cost assumptions

  • Payment processing: ~3%
  • Refund reserve: 5%
  • Marketing tools: ~$200/month
  • Assistant time for admin: covered by existing team
  • Regina's time: 4–6 hours per week during cohort

Unit economics at $1,295 founding price

  • 12 paying students × $1,295 = $15,540 gross
  • Minus payment fees (~$466) and refunds (~$777) = ~$14,297 net
  • Net per student: ~$1,191

Target Audience

Primary audience

Serious aspiring memoir writers with a meaningful personal story and some professional or public platform.

Psychographic profile

  • Age 35–60
  • Has lived through something worth documenting
  • May be a professional, founder, educator, community leader, or creative
  • Wants to do the work, not just dream about writing
  • Values expert guidance and publishing industry credibility
  • Willing to invest $1,000+ for the right program

Secondary audience

Published or platform-building authors who want to understand memoir positioning and marketing.

Launch Operating Sequence

Course strategy → Demand validation → Pilot launch → Enrollment → Delivery → Marketing → Improvement

Phase 1: Course strategy (Week 1)

  • Confirm audience, promise, curriculum, and price with Regina.
  • Audit existing course materials and IP ownership.
  • Define success metrics.

Phase 2: Demand validation (Weeks 2–4)

  • Build a waitlist landing page.
  • Send announcement to Regina's email list and LinkedIn.
  • Publish 5–7 organic pieces about memoir writing and publishing.
  • Collect waitlist signups and questions.

Phase 3: Pilot launch (Weeks 5–6)

  • Open applications.
  • Run 3-email waitlist launch sequence.
  • Host one free Q&A or mini-workshop.
  • Review applications manually.

Phase 4: Enrollment (Week 7)

  • Send acceptances and payment links.
  • Onboard the founding cohort.

Phase 5: Delivery (Weeks 8–13)

  • Run live sessions.
  • Collect weekly feedback and testimonials.
  • Track completion and satisfaction.

Phase 6: Marketing (Weeks 14–16)

  • Use testimonials and results to promote the next cohort.
  • Open public enrollment.

Phase 7: Improvement (ongoing)

  • Refine curriculum, pricing, and automation based on data.

Conversion benchmarks

  • Waitlist to application: 15–25%
  • Application to paid: 50–70%

Application and Selection System

Application form fields

1. Name and email 2. What is your memoir about in 2–3 sentences? 3. Where are you in the writing process? 4. Why do you want to join this program now? 5. What is your biggest obstacle? 6. Can you attend live sessions and complete weekly work? 7. What do you hope to leave with after six weeks?

Review process

  • AI summarizes applications and flags fit issues.
  • Regina or assistant reviews each application.
  • Regina makes final acceptance decisions.
  • Unsuitable applicants receive a redirect to a free resource.

AI Boundaries

AI supports

  • Drafting social posts, emails, and landing page copy for human approval
  • Summarizing applications
  • Repurposing long-form content
  • Building simple dashboards
  • Scheduling approved content

AI does not

  • Publish anything without Regina's approval
  • Make final acceptance decisions
  • Handle personal author communication
  • Write legal, publishing, or financial advice
  • Respond directly to students or applicants

Risk Guardrails

  • Do not promise book deals, bestseller status, or guaranteed publication.
  • Keep all claims tied to skills, outputs, and knowledge gained.
  • Make results dependent on participant effort.
  • Avoid aggressive scarcity that could damage reputation.
  • Keep application process supportive, not elitist.
  • Verify IP ownership of any Writer's Digest materials before reuse.
