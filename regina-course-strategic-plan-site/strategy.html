1. Actual Problem and Desired Outcome

Confirmed facts

  • Regina Brooks is the founder of Serendipity Literary Agency.
  • She wants to create a course connected to her book and existing expertise.
  • Proposed course title: You Should Really Write a Book: How to Write, Sell, and Market Your Memoir.
  • She has already taught this subject through platforms such as Writer's Digest.
  • People have encouraged her to create and own her own course.
  • She wants to automate the marketing of the course.
  • She may want an automated application, qualification, or participant-selection process.
  • The author community is sensitive to AI, so human relationships and sensitive communication must remain human-led.

Working assumptions to validate with Regina

  • Regina wants to move from teaching on third-party platforms to owning the course herself.
  • She has enough authority and credibility to attract serious memoir writers.
  • Her existing audience and network can generate the first cohort without paid ads.
  • A live pilot is the best way to prove demand before building a full recorded course.
  • She or her assistant can commit 4–6 hours per week during the pilot.

Desired outcome

Launch her own course business, reduce manual marketing and enrollment work, and maintain control and trust within the author community.

2. Strengths of the Proposed Approach

  • Validates demand before building a large recorded course.
  • Starts with a paid pilot instead of over-investing in infrastructure.
  • Records live pilot sessions so proven material can later scale.
  • Keeps Regina and her assistant in control of brand voice and participant selection.
  • Uses AI behind the scenes rather than replacing human judgment.
  • Phases the work so each step informs the next.

3. Weaknesses, Premature Elements, and Gaps

  • The Skillstream example is unvalidated. An application process may not be needed for a first pilot.
  • Phase 4 AI-assisted marketing may be too much too soon. A first pilot may only need a landing page, a waitlist form, and Regina promoting to her network.
  • Existing course material ownership (for example from Writer's Digest) has not been verified.
  • The exact size and engagement of Regina's audience are unknown.
  • The final price depends on audience validation.
  • A complex AI selection agent is unnecessary at the pilot stage.

4. Recommended Solution

Core operating sequence

Course strategy → Demand validation → Pilot launch → Enrollment → Delivery → Marketing → Improvement

This sequence means we design the course first, validate that people want it, run a small pilot, enroll the right participants, deliver the program, market it based on real outcomes, and then improve each cycle.

Simplified first path

1. Course strategy — define audience, promise, format, and price. 2. Demand validation — build a landing page with an interest or waitlist form. 3. Regina promotes the waitlist to her existing audience. 4. Pilot launch — if demand is proven, open a small paid live cohort. 5. Enrollment — accept applications, collect payment, onboard participants. 6. Delivery — run the live pilot, collect outcomes and testimonials. 7. Marketing — use pilot results to promote the next cohort. 8. Improvement — refine curriculum, pricing, and automation based on data. 9. Only then design the full application process, growing course, and advanced automation.

7. Discovery Answers — Working Brief

The following answers are working assumptions based on a recommended brief. They must still be confirmed by Regina.

1. Ideal participant

Aspiring or early-stage memoir writers who have a meaningful personal story but need expert guidance to shape it into a marketable book. Likely professionals, founders, community leaders, educators, creatives, or people with significant lived experience. They should be serious, able to complete assignments, and interested in writing, publishing, and marketing — not only the writing part.

2. Participant outcome

By the end of the first program, participants should leave with:

  • A clearly defined memoir concept
  • A specific target reader
  • The central promise and theme of the book
  • A structured chapter outline
  • One completed or substantially improved sample chapter
  • A realistic writing and completion plan
  • An understanding of relevant publishing options
  • A preliminary author-platform and book-marketing plan
  • Clear next steps for completing and bringing the memoir to market

The course should not promise that participants will fully publish and market a memoir in six weeks.

3. Course format

Begin with a six-week live pilot cohort.

  • One 90-minute live session per week
  • Weekly exercises and templates
  • Private online participant community
  • Session recordings for participants
  • One group feedback or office-hours session each week or every two weeks
  • Optional premium individual consultation

After one or two successful cohorts, convert proven material into a hybrid or self-paced course.

6. Pricing approach

Hypothesis: Founding cohort price of $795.

Possible future pricing:

  • Standard cohort price after validation: $1,095 to $1,295
  • Premium tier with individual consultation: $1,495 or above

This assumes live teaching, feedback, templates, and direct access to Regina. The final price depends on duration, audience purchasing power, competition, and demand.

7. First cohort size

Begin with 12 to 15 paid participants.

This is large enough to validate demand, generate revenue, collect feedback, and produce testimonials — while staying small enough for personal interaction.

8. Application and selection process

An application process is useful only if the program depends on participant quality, readiness, or fit.

Pilot recommendation: Use a short qualification form followed by human approval. A complex AI selection agent is unnecessary.

Possible objectives of the form:

  • Confirm applicants are serious
  • Identify each applicant's memoir idea and current stage
  • Determine whether the course matches the applicant's needs
  • Protect cohort quality
  • Identify sensitive legal, ethical, or privacy issues
  • Direct unsuitable applicants to a more appropriate resource

AI may summarize applications and flag fit issues, but Regina should make final acceptance decisions.

9. Regina's time commitment

Recommended minimum: 4–6 hours per week during the pilot.

Possible weekly allocation:

  • 90 minutes for live teaching
  • 60 minutes for preparation
  • 60–90 minutes for office hours or feedback
  • 30 minutes for content approval
  • 30 minutes for reviewing applications or participant issues

Rajesh and Regina's assistant can manage administration, content repurposing, scheduling, reporting, and coordination.

Regina must confirm whether this commitment is realistic.

10. First launch success criteria

Treat the first launch as demand and delivery validation, not only a revenue campaign.

Working targets:
  • 12 to 15 paid participants
  • At least 80% program completion
  • At least 75% of participants completing their memoir outline
  • At least 60% producing a sample chapter or equivalent deliverable
  • Average participant satisfaction of at least 8 out of 10
  • Five or more credible testimonials
  • Clear evidence identifying which lessons and exercises produce results
  • A refined curriculum ready for the next cohort
  • A documented and repeatable enrollment and delivery process
  • Enough revenue to cover delivery and launch costs

These targets should be adjusted after Regina confirms the format, audience, price, and level of support.

8. Recommended Proposal Structure for Regina

1. Confirm the goal: own and launch her own course. 2. State what will not be built first: a full recorded course or complete automation. 3. Propose the smallest first step: strategy session + waitlist page. 4. Explain the pilot approach: six-week live cohort, 12–15 participants, $795 founding price. 5. Show the human-AI boundary: AI supports drafting and admin; Regina approves all public-facing content and participant selection. 6. Define what success looks like: waitlist size, pilot signups, completion rates, testimonials. 7. Close with the next step: schedule a 90-minute strategy call.

10. Major Risks

Commercial

  • Unknown demand. The course may not attract enough paying students.
  • Pricing may be wrong if competitive research is skipped.
  • The audience may be too broad for effective positioning.

Operational

  • Regina's assistant is already busy with operations and author communication.
  • Adding course operations may overload the team.
  • The 4–6 hour weekly commitment may be too much for Regina's schedule.

Ethical and reputational

  • AI-generated content that feels impersonal or off-brand could damage trust in the author community.
  • A poorly designed application process could feel elitist and reduce conversions.

Legal and IP

  • Writer's Digest or other platforms may own rights to past course material.
  • Reusing that material without checking could create problems.

Selection process

  • Building an application system before demand exists adds unnecessary friction.
  • Final participant decisions should remain human to protect reputation.
